\begin{abstract}
We give a FRI-based test of the total degree, and more generally of any weighted degree, of
a multivariate polynomial committed through its univariate Kronecker image, the encoding
used by FRI-based commitments to multilinear and higher-degree polynomials. The test is FRI
with symbolic folding challenges $r_iT^{w_i}$: the oracles take values in $\F[T]$, and the
last message is $g(T)=f(r_1T^{w_1},\dots,r_mT^{w_m})$, whose degree is the weighted degree
of $f$. Existing tests either certify only the individual degrees fixed by the encoding or
work on evaluations over a product set, with $2^m$ queries per round; ours runs on the
univariate encoding and reads at most $km(D+1)$ field elements per query for $k$-ary folding
and degree bound $D$. Soundness holds up to the Johnson radius. The proof adapts the FRI
analysis of Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Ishai, Kopparty and Saraf through two observations: their
correlated-agreement theorems remain valid when the code lives over an extension of the
challenge field, and weighted correlated agreement over curves passes to every linear
subcode, so the test applies to linear subcodes of Reed--Solomon codes in general, such as the
constrained codes of WHIR. For $N=2^{20}$, rate $2^{-4}$, eight variables of individual degree below $4$ and
total degree at most $6$, $65$ query repetitions over a $256$-bit field give soundness error
below $2^{-129.9}$. A Rust prototype checks completeness and the predicted behaviour on
cheating provers.
\end{abstract}
